Fully backed, checkable on-chain.

Every AIUSD is backed by one USDC held in its own contract. On each network, the contract's USDC balance equals AIUSD supply, and the contract enforces it on every mint and redemption.

AIUSD is minted only when USDC is deposited, and the USDC leaves only to pay a redemption. There is no admin key for either, Loka included. The reserve is never lent, invested or used to cover loan losses.

Supply and reserve are both on-chain, so anyone can check them at any block instead of waiting for a monthly report. Loka also reconciles bank, USDC and AIUSD balances daily and sends each lender a monthly statement.

An independent audit before launch and before every upgrade. Upgrades go through a multisig with a time lock and are announced in advance.

How a dollar moves.

From a company bank account into a loan and back, at 1:1 at every step.

  1. Fund

    Deposit USDC from a registered wallet, or wire USD from your company account and a licensed partner converts it to USDC.

    USD → USDC (partner)
  2. Mint

    The USDC goes into the AIUSD contract and the same amount of AIUSD is minted to your account.

    USDC → AIUSD contract
  3. Lend

    Commit AIUSD to a loan. It waits in that loan's escrow until the drawdown conditions are met.

    Escrow · Loka Capital
  4. Get repaid, lend again

    Monthly repayments land in your AIUSD balance. Lend them to the next loan, or redeem 1:1 for USDC or USD to your registered account.

    AIUSD → USDC / USD

Why loans settle in AIUSD.

Loka Capital

Every loan on Loka Capital is denominated in AIUSD, so each step of a loan happens in contracts anyone can check: who committed, what is held in reserve, and how each month’s rent was split.

Questions.

What is AIUSD?

The dollar Loka Capital loans settle in. It is redeemable 1:1 for USDC, and loans, escrow, reserves and repayments on Loka Capital are all denominated in it.

How do I get AIUSD?

Verify your business, then wire USD from a company account with your reference code, or deposit USDC from a registered wallet. Either way, AIUSD is minted 1:1 into your account.

Which networks is AIUSD on?

Ethereum, Base and Arbitrum. Each network has its own AIUSD contract backed by native USDC on that network. You mint and redeem on the same network, and each loan runs on one network, shown on its page.

Do I mint for every loan?

No. AIUSD stays in your balance and can be committed to several loans over time. Repayments and refunds land back in the same balance, so you can lend them again without another wire, or redeem them.

Can I always redeem it?

AIUSD in your account can be redeemed 1:1 for USDC, or for USD sent to your registered company account. AIUSD committed to a loan stays in that loan's escrow and follows the loan's terms.

What are the risks?

AIUSD is only as sound as USDC, the stablecoin backing it, and the AIUSD contract itself, which is audited before launch. AIUSD committed to a loan carries that loan's credit risk until it is repaid.

Does AIUSD pay interest?

No. Holding AIUSD earns nothing. Returns come from lending it on Loka Capital, at each loan's fixed rate.

Does holding AIUSD expose me to loans?

No. The USDC behind AIUSD is never lent or used to cover loan losses. Only AIUSD you commit to a specific loan carries that loan’s risk.

What do borrowers gain from settling in AIUSD?

Proposed: a lower rate for borrowers who keep extra reserve in AIUSD, or whose tenants pay rent straight into the loan’s collection contract. Cash that is on-chain from the moment it arrives is visible to lenders and controlled by the contract. The size of any reduction is set in each loan’s terms.

Who issues AIUSD and where is the reserve?

AIUSD is issued by its own contract, deployed by Loka. The reserve is the USDC locked in that contract. AIUSD can only be minted when USDC is deposited, and the USDC can only leave to pay a redemption.
